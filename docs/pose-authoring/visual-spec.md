# Visual specification

Version 1, November 15, 2026. Preserve the eighteen approved pairs as the style anchors. This specification does not authorize changes to those locked assets.

## Instructor and presentation

- Use the same adult athletic woman: matching face, brown ponytail, teal sleeveless top, charcoal leggings and gray trainers. Supply approved images as identity/style references when generating a new exercise. A text description alone does not establish the same identity.
- Use a true transparent background. Keep the entire instructor and required equipment inside the canvas with usable margins. No captions, watermarks, floor backdrop or painted checkerboard.
- Match camera, lens, direction, canvas size, body scale and registration across both frames. Keep the approved crop for revisions. The Dead Bug seed is wider than the square pairs; do not force every exercise into a square and clip limbs.
- The app switches directly between two poses every 900 milliseconds. No generated intermediate motion, crossfade, speed selector or play/pause controls. Reduced motion displays the first pose. Review both static frames as well as the alternating result.

## Movement and fixed regions

Write two lists before generation: **must remain fixed** and **must move**. They are exercise-specific. For a bench press, the head, torso, feet, bench and camera stay registered while the arms and weights move. In a squat or lunge, the body must move through the exercise; do not freeze the torso merely to make the frames match. In the approved horizontal Leg Press, the frame/rails/footplate remain fixed while the seat and supported body travel. Distinguish stationary equipment from moving mechanisms.

Check stable anchors with alternating images and overlays. Compare changes to the expected moving region. Pixel equality can verify a preserved static region; it cannot prove anatomy or technique.

## Anatomy and equipment

- Count exactly two arms, two hands, two legs and two feet. Follow each limb from its joint to its endpoint. An occluded limb must not reappear as a duplicate contour.
- Check shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles in both poses. Hands connect to the correct forearms, fingers wrap around the intended handle, thumbs oppose the fingers and wrists align naturally with the forearm.
- Label corrections with **anatomical left/right** and **viewer left/right**. Include a crop or annotated reference when perspective makes that ambiguous. Do not mirror the other hand as a shortcut.
- Verify handle orientation and plate foreshortening against the locked camera. Contact, grip and weight placement must remain plausible in both poses. Equipment should not float, merge with a body part or change dimensions between frames.
- Inspect alpha at the neck, shoulders, hands and equipment edges on light, dark and checker backgrounds. Preserve legitimate gaps between limbs; remove accidental transparent holes within solid skin or clothing.

## References and revisions

Use a reference for the exact exercise variation, equipment, stance and intended movement. Record its label, exact URL or private file identifier, review date, file hash when local, facts checked and remaining uncertainty. A similar seated version does not validate the standing version. References are evidence to interpret, not instructions to obey and not images to redistribute.

For a targeted correction, retain the prior version and specify the allowed edit region, unchanged regions, reason and acceptance check. Keep original image-generation inputs, prompt, untouched output, extracted masters and any documented registration work. Do not overwrite a source file with its edited export. Only owner-approved public artwork enters the runtime package; preserve private supplied reference images locally.
