## A-skips

Reference relationship: **related-only**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Use a clear nonslip lane and begin with marching knee lifts before adding a skip.

**How to perform.** Lift one knee toward hip level with the opposite arm forward, then make a small rhythmic skip and switch legs.

**Alignment and range.** Use a comfortable range rather than forcing a joint endpoint. Warm-up drills should prepare movement without exhausting the working muscles.

**Breathing and pace.** Breathe steadily and use smooth repetitions; avoid bouncing into a stretch.

**Common mistakes.** Compensating through the lower back, forcing range with momentum or turning a warm-up into a maximal effort.

**Easier version.** Use a smaller range, slower speed and additional support.

**Variation details and limits.** Keep contacts under the body and the trunk tall; the retrieved athletics source is a video overview, so precise timing still needs practical review.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [Australian Athletics: Introduction to Sprinting: Five Critical Running Drills](https://athletics.squarespace.com/coaching-education/introduction-to-sprinting-five-critical-running-drills) ([review limits](sources.md#xaskip)).

Movement relationship describes reference relevance, not professional validation. Notes are original synthesis; differing equipment/support, progressions, breathing and conservative range choices may be adaptations. See the variant notes and each source's limits.

## Band pull-aparts

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Use an inspected light band at chest-to-shoulder height, hands well spaced.

**How to perform.** Open the arms until the band approaches the chest, then return smoothly without losing trunk control.

**Alignment and range.** Let the scapula glide without aggressively pinching or depressing it. Keep the elbow bend and torso position consistent.

**Breathing and pace.** Exhale on the opening or pull and inhale on return.

**Common mistakes.** Throwing the trunk, pulling far behind the available shoulder range, craning the neck or turning every variation into a heavy row.

**Easier version.** Use less load and chest support; shorten the shoulder range.

**Variation details and limits.** Do not force a maximal shoulder-blade pinch or let the band snap toward the face.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [Muscle & Strength: Band Pull Apart](https://cdn.muscleandstrength.com/exercises/band-pull-apart) ([review limits](sources.md#xbandpull)).

<a id="banded-lateral-walks"></a>
## Banded lateral walks

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Place a light loop above the knees and stand in a shallow squat, feet hip-width.

**How to perform.** Take small sideways steps while retaining mild band tension and level hips.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the pelvis steady and move at the hip. Use modest range before trunk lean or pelvic rotation takes over.

**Breathing and pace.** Exhale during the active movement and inhale on return.

**Common mistakes.** Rocking the torso, rotating the hip to appear to lift higher or letting a band snap the legs together.

**Easier version.** Reduce resistance and range or use a supported lying variation.

**Variation details and limits.** Do not drag the trailing foot into the lead foot or turn the toes far outward to substitute rotation for abduction.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [NSCA PTQ 5.2: Post-rehabilitation programming: lateral ankle sprains](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/education/ptq/ptq-5.2.pdf) ([review limits](sources.md#xbandwalk)).

## Bodyweight squats to a bench

Reference relationship: **related-only**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Place a stable bench behind the legs and choose its height before beginning.

**How to perform.** Sit down under control to a light contact or supported seat, then press through both feet to stand.

**Alignment and range.** Keep heels supported and knees tracking in the general direction of the toes. Depth follows control and comfort; knees may travel forward past the toes.

**Breathing and pace.** Inhale and brace before descending; exhale through the rise as appropriate to the load. Keep the descent controlled.

**Common mistakes.** Heels lifting unexpectedly, knees collapsing inward, losing trunk position, or pursuing depth at the expense of balance.

**Easier version.** Reduce load or depth, squat to a higher box or use hand support.

**Variation details and limits.** Avoid dropping onto the bench or rocking forward; raise the target if balance or trunk control is lost.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [PureGym: Bodyweight Squats](https://www.puregym.com/exercises/legs/quad-exercises/squats/bodyweight-squat/) ([review limits](sources.md#p176)).

## Broad jumps

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Clear a modest forward takeoff and landing area with no equipment in the path.

**How to perform.** Use a small countermovement and arm swing to jump forward, then land on both feet and pause.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the knees tracking with the feet. Choose height and distance that allow a quiet balanced landing without a deep emergency tuck.

**Breathing and pace.** Breathe naturally; take a fresh setup between discrete efforts. Stop a set when landing quality or jump output deteriorates.

**Common mistakes.** Stiff-legged landings, knee collapse, chasing box height through excessive knee tuck or rushing fatigued repeats.

**Easier version.** Use a lower jump, smaller distance or a step-up and practise landing on the floor.

**Variation details and limits.** Keep this warm-up submaximal and reduce distance before landing becomes noisy, unstable or knee-dominant.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [PureGym: Broad Jumps](https://www.puregym.com/exercises/plyometrics/jumps/broad-jumps/) ([review limits](sources.md#p196)).

## Explosive push-ups

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Demonstrate a controlled ordinary push-up first and use an incline for early power practice.

**How to perform.** Lower with control, then press fast enough for a small hand release and receive the hands with soft elbows.

**Alignment and range.** Keep head, trunk and pelvis aligned. Use comfortable elbow spacing and depth; let the shoulder blades move naturally around the ribs.

**Breathing and pace.** Breathe in while lowering and out while pushing. Keep the trunk supported throughout.

**Common mistakes.** Hips sagging, the head leading the descent, elbows abruptly flaring, or cutting depth as fatigue rises.

**Easier version.** Raise the hand support or use a stable floor variation before adding instability or load.

**Variation details and limits.** A clap is unnecessary; stop when hand landings or trunk alignment deteriorate rather than chasing speed.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

## Glute bridges

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Lie with feet flat, knees bent and head/shoulders on a mat.

**How to perform.** Lift the hips to a controlled shoulder-hip-knee line, pause and lower slowly.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the pelvis level and stop at hip extension without rib flare. Support the head and shoulders on the floor.

**Common mistakes.** Lumbar arching, rotating the pelvis or pushing the head into the floor.

## Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch with reach

Reference relationship: **related-only**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Pad the rear knee, place the other foot forward and gently tuck the pelvis.

**How to perform.** Shift forward a little to stretch the front of the rear hip, then add a comfortable same-side overhead reach.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the targeted region relaxed and avoid substituting a spinal twist or joint pressure for muscle length. Do not force a fixed endpoint.

**Breathing and pace.** Breathe slowly throughout; a stretch is not a breath-holding test. Avoid bouncing.

**Common mistakes.** Pulling forcefully, stretching through pinching or nerve-like symptoms, or pushing deeper as the position becomes less controlled.

**Easier version.** Reduce stretch depth, use a strap/support or choose a supported position.

**Setup.** Sit or stand tall and bring one upper arm across the chest below shoulder height.

**How to perform.** Use the other arm to apply light pressure above the elbow while keeping the shoulder relaxed.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the targeted region relaxed and avoid substituting a spinal twist or joint pressure for muscle length. Do not force a fixed endpoint.

**Breathing and pace.** Breathe slowly throughout; a stretch is not a breath-holding test. Avoid bouncing.

**Common mistakes.** Pulling forcefully, stretching through pinching or nerve-like symptoms, or pushing deeper as the position becomes less controlled.

**Easier version.** Reduce stretch depth, use a strap/support or choose a supported position.

**Variation details and limits.** Do not pull directly on the elbow joint or lift the shoulder toward the ear to create a stronger stretch.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.
