## Barbell Bench Press

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Lie with eyes near the racked bar, head/upper back/pelvis supported and feet planted. Set hooks below full arm reach and safeties just below the planned bottom. Wrap thumbs around the bar.

**How to perform.** Unrack over the shoulders, lower toward the mid-to-lower chest with forearms near vertical, then press slightly back toward the rack without contacting it.

**Alignment and range.** Keep wrists near the forearm line and forearms approximately under the load. Choose an elbow angle and bottom depth that keep the shoulder comfortable; do not force a fixed angle.

**Breathing and pace.** Inhale before or during lowering; maintain trunk support and exhale through the press. Keep the lowering smooth rather than bouncing.

**Common mistakes.** Bouncing off the chest, wrists folding back, shoulders rolling forward at the bottom, or lifting the pelvis to finish.

**Easier version.** Use a lighter load, a shorter comfortable range or a floor press. Set up rack safeties and a competent spotter for a barbell.

**Variation details and limits.** A moderate natural arch is acceptable with the pelvis supported; do not bounce the bar or rely on the spotter for repetitions.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [PureGym: Barbell Bench Press](https://www.puregym.com/exercises/chest/bench-press/barbell-bench-press/) ([review limits](sources.md#p83)); [NASM: Barbell Bench Press](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/barbell-bench-press) ([review limits](sources.md#nbench)).

## Machine Chest Press

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Adjust the seat so the handles begin around mid-chest; set the starting range before taking the load.

**How to perform.** Press forward with the back against the pad, then return until the upper arms reach a comfortable chest-level stretch.

**Alignment and range.** Keep wrists near the forearm line and forearms approximately under the load. Choose an elbow angle and bottom depth that keep the shoulder comfortable; do not force a fixed angle.

**Breathing and pace.** Inhale before or during lowering; maintain trunk support and exhale through the press. Keep the lowering smooth rather than bouncing.

**Common mistakes.** Seat too low or high, pelvis sliding forward, shoulders rolling at the bottom or weight-stack impact.

**Easier version.** Reduce the machine load and starting depth; use its supported bilateral handles before an independent-arm version.

**Variation details and limits.** Do not slide forward, flare the ribs or let the handles force the shoulders behind their available range.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

<a id="standing-cable-chest-press"></a>
## Standing Cable Chest Press

Reference relationship: **related-only**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Set two pulleys around chest level, take the handles and step into a stable staggered stance.

**How to perform.** Press both handles forward while maintaining balance against the backward pull; allow the shoulder blades to wrap around the ribs.

**Alignment and range.** Keep ribs over the pelvis and feet planted. Allow controlled shoulder-blade movement without twisting the trunk to finish.

**Breathing and pace.** Exhale during the press; inhale on the return. Make both directions deliberate.

**Common mistakes.** Leaning the whole body into the load, arching the back, or letting the handles pull the shoulders abruptly backward.

**Easier version.** Use less resistance and a wider base, or use a supported chest press.

**Variation details and limits.** This is a standing cable adaptation of pressing mechanics; do not treat the machine's back-pad instructions as part of this version.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [PureGym: Seated Chest Press (Machine Chest Press)](https://www.puregym.com/exercises/chest/bench-press/seated-chest-press/) ([review limits](sources.md#p91)).

## Weighted Push-Up

Reference relationship: **related-only**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Secure a vest or other purpose-made load before assuming the plank; confirm the load cannot shift toward the neck.

**How to perform.** Lower and raise the body as one unit with the added load stable over the torso.

**Alignment and range.** Keep head, trunk and pelvis aligned. Use comfortable elbow spacing and depth; let the shoulder blades move naturally around the ribs.

**Breathing and pace.** Breathe in while lowering and out while pushing. Keep the trunk supported throughout.

**Common mistakes.** Hips sagging, the head leading the descent, elbows abruptly flaring, or cutting depth as fatigue rises.

**Easier version.** Raise the hand support or use a stable floor variation before adding instability or load.

**Variation details and limits.** Use a competent helper for a plate; avoid loose loads that can slide during entry, fatigue or exit.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

## Weighted Dip

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Attach load with a secure belt or vest and use a step to enter the support position without jumping.

**How to perform.** Lower with a small controlled forward torso inclination and press back up without swinging the suspended load.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the shoulders supported and use only the depth available without front-shoulder discomfort. A right angle is a reference, not a mandatory bottom position.

**Breathing and pace.** Inhale as you lower and exhale as you rise; avoid dropping into the bottom.

**Common mistakes.** Shoulders collapsing upward, forcing deep shoulder extension, swinging legs, or adding load before bodyweight control.

**Easier version.** Use an assisted dip or a close-grip incline push-up; reduce the bottom range.

**Variation details and limits.** Add load only after repeatable bodyweight dips; shoulder comfort determines the bottom, not an obligation to descend below the elbows.
