## Kettlebell Windmill

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Set a very light bell securely overhead and turn the feet slightly away from the loaded side as comfortable.

**How to perform.** Push the loaded-side hip back while the free hand slides down the other leg; keep the bell supported and return tall.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the load close and knee bend modest. Stop when further hip travel would require a spinal position change; reaching the floor is unnecessary.

**Breathing and pace.** Inhale and brace on lowering, then exhale during the return. Use controlled lowering.

**Common mistakes.** Converting the movement to a squat, rounding to reach lower, or thrusting the pelvis beyond a tall finish.

**Easier version.** Use less load, a shorter range or practise touching the hips to a wall.

**Variation details and limits.** Depth depends on hip mobility and shoulder control; floor contact is optional and the elbow must not buckle under the bell.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [ACE: High Windmill](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/386/high-windmill/) ([review limits](sources.md#xwindmill)).

Movement relationship describes reference relevance, not professional validation. Notes are original synthesis; differing equipment/support, progressions, breathing and conservative range choices may be adaptations. See the variant notes and each source's limits.

<a id="barbell-back-squat"></a>
## Barbell Back Squat

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Set hooks below shoulder height, safeties below the intended bottom and the bar on an appropriate muscular shelf, not the neck.

**How to perform.** Unrack with both feet under the bar, take a short walkout, squat with balanced foot pressure and stand before re-racking.

**Alignment and range.** Keep heels supported and knees tracking in the general direction of the toes. Depth follows control and comfort; knees may travel forward past the toes.

**Breathing and pace.** Inhale and brace before descending; exhale through the rise as appropriate to the load. Keep the descent controlled.

**Common mistakes.** Heels lifting unexpectedly, knees collapsing inward, losing trunk position, or pursuing depth at the expense of balance.

**Easier version.** Reduce load or depth, squat to a higher box or use hand support.

**Variation details and limits.** Choose high- or low-bar placement deliberately; do not carry a loaded bar over the head to improvise the setup.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [PureGym: Barbell Back Squats](https://www.puregym.com/exercises/legs/quad-exercises/squats/barbell-back-squat/) ([review limits](sources.md#p175)).

**How to perform.** Keep elbows forward as the knees and hips bend, then stand without letting the bar roll into the hands.

**Alignment and range.** Keep heels supported and knees tracking in the general direction of the toes. Depth follows control and comfort; knees may travel forward past the toes.

<a id="leg-press"></a>
## Leg Press

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Use the actual machine's safety catches, backrest and seat adjustments; place both feet fully on the plate.

**How to perform.** Lower with the pelvis still against the pad and press back without knee snap or hands pushing on the thighs.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the pelvis and back supported. End the descent before the pelvis rolls off the pad or the heels lift.

**Breathing and pace.** Inhale during lowering and exhale through the press. Do not bounce off the bottom stops.

**Common mistakes.** Excessive depth with the pelvis rolling, pushing on the knees with the hands or losing foot contact.

**Easier version.** Reduce resistance and depth; use both legs before progressing to single-leg work.

**Variation details and limits.** A universal knee angle or maximum depth is inappropriate; stop before pelvic motion even if the carriage could move farther.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [PureGym: 45 Degree Leg Press](https://www.puregym.com/exercises/legs/quad-exercises/leg-presses/45-degree-leg-press/) ([review limits](sources.md#p163)); [NASM: Leg Press](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/leg-press) ([review limits](sources.md#nleg-press)).

<a id="conventional-deadlift"></a>
## Conventional Deadlift

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Set the bar over the midfoot, hands outside the legs and shoulders positioned for a close vertical bar path.

**How to perform.** Take slack out before pushing the floor away; extend hips and knees together, then hinge to return the bar past the knees.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the load close and arms long. Finish standing tall without leaning back; maintain a consistent controlled spinal position.

**Breathing and pace.** Breathe and brace before the pull, exhale as the effort permits and reset at the bottom. Avoid prolonged breath holding.

**Common mistakes.** Yanking a slack load, hips shooting up before the load moves, the load drifting forward or leaning back at lockout.

**Easier version.** Raise the starting load on blocks or use a lighter kettlebell deadlift.

**Variation details and limits.** Do not force exaggerated shoulder-blade pinching or a universal hip height; keep the arms long and the bar near the shins.

**When to stop or regress.** End or regress when the described alignment, support or breathing is lost. Stop for sharp pain, pinching, numbness or tingling; individual symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

**References reviewed.** [PureGym: Conventional Deadlifts](https://www.puregym.com/exercises/legs/hamstring-exercises/deadlifts/conventional-deadlift/) ([review limits](sources.md#p138)); [NASM: Barbell Deadlift](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/barbell-deadlift) ([review limits](sources.md#ndeadlift)).

## Romanian Deadlift

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Begin standing with a bar held near the thighs, knees softly bent and grip outside the legs.

**How to perform.** Push the hips backward while the bar remains near the legs; stand when hamstring range ends without reaching for the floor.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the load close and knee bend modest. Stop when further hip travel would require a spinal position change; reaching the floor is unnecessary.

**Breathing and pace.** Inhale and brace on lowering, then exhale during the return. Use controlled lowering.

**Common mistakes.** Converting the movement to a squat, rounding to reach lower, or thrusting the pelvis beyond a tall finish.

**Easier version.** Use less load, a shorter range or practise touching the hips to a wall.

## Back Extension

Reference relationship: **same-movement**. Status: internal research draft.

**Setup.** Set the hip pad below the hip crease with heels secured and room for the torso to hinge.

**How to perform.** Fold at the hips and return to an approximately straight body line with glute and trunk control.

**Alignment and range.** Keep the load close and knee bend modest. Stop when further hip travel would require a spinal position change; reaching the floor is unnecessary.

**Breathing and pace.** Inhale and brace on lowering, then exhale during the return. Use controlled lowering.

**Easier version.** Use less load, a shorter range or practise touching the hips to a wall.

**Variation details and limits.** This is a hip-hinge baseline, not repeated lumbar hyperextension; do not lift the chest beyond the leg line to chase more height.
